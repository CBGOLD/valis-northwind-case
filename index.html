2 / One number for the CFO

$7,500 this quarter — one invoice decides what it means.

Baseline, arithmetic, what's unverified, and the decision it unlocks — built to survive a finance review.

Either a $7,500 bookkeeping fix, or $30,000/year of duplicate software to cancel.

Finance's books show $81,000 spent on software in Q1 2026 — that's the baseline, and it's exact: 15 line items sum to the stated subtotal. Two of those lines — "Amplitude" and "Amplitude Analytics" — are $7,500 each, same note, posted back to back. That looks like one bill entered twice. Finance is 90% sure; nobody has pulled the invoice to be certain.

The arithmetic:

Booked Q1 SaaS = $81,000
− suspected duplicate = −$7,500
Defensible Q1 SaaS, if confirmed = $73,500
Share of the published line = $7,500 / $81,000 = 9.3%
If it recurs every quarter = $7,500 × 4 = $30,000/year

What's not verified: the Amplitude invoice itself (not in the bundle, requested June 2, never returned); whether this repeats past Q1; whether any cash actually left twice (if it's one invoice posted twice, cash recovery is $0); and the ~10% chance these are genuinely two different products, which nothing in the data confirms or rules out.

The decision this enables: pull that one invoice. If it's one contract billed twice, correct the books by $7,500 — no cash to chase, just an accurate number. If it's two real contracts, cancel $30,000/year of duplicate tooling and rename both line items so this can't happen silently again. Either way, the CFO gets a bounded number today instead of an open question.

Advanced: how it works, the stress test, and the export

What goes in

Three files joined on a deal ID.

  • Closed deals: amount, close date, creator split
  • Invoices: billed amount and date
  • Payouts: paid amount and creator

What Run does

Checks IDs, groups every row, compares amount/date/split rules, and gives every deal ID it sees exactly one outcome: cleared, or sent to the review list with a reason.

How to read the result

Money check proves no dollar was lost or invented: the total of closed deals equals cleared dollars plus flagged dollars. Every deal accounted for proves every deal ID seen was either cleared or put on the review list — none silently dropped.
